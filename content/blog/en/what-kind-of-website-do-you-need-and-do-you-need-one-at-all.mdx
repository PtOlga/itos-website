When a business owner says, "I need a website," I always ask one question: what should it do? It sounds obvious, but this is exactly where the difference appears — between a website that works for you and one that simply exists.

## Brochure website: "I exist"

The simplest option. A few pages: who you are, what you do, how to contact you. No complexity, no automation.

**Good fit if:** you are a freelancer, a tradesperson, or a small local business — and the most important thing is to show up on Google or have a link to share.\
**Not a good fit if:** you want the website to bring in customers on its own.

## Landing page: one goal — one action

One page, one call to action: book, buy, send an inquiry. Everything else is removed so nothing distracts the visitor.

**Good fit if:** you are launching a specific service or product, running ads, or promoting a campaign.\
**Not a good fit if:** you offer many services or want to present the company as a whole.

## Company website: trust and information

Several sections: about the company, services, team, references, contact. The goal is not to sell immediately — but to convince people that you are reliable.

**Good fit if:** your customers take time to decide and want to get to know you before they call.\
**Not a good fit if:** you need fast sales — the customer journey here is longer.

## Online store: selling online

Product catalog, cart, payment, delivery. It sounds simple, but behind it there is a whole system: stock levels, notifications, returns, and accounting integrations.

**Good fit if:** you sell physical or digital products and want to accept orders online.\
**Not a good fit if:** you only have 3–5 products — in that case a landing page with an order form is often enough.

## Blog and content website: expertise and traffic

Articles, tips, guides — content that people find through search engines. It is a long-term investment: you write, and then the website brings in visitors on its own.

**Good fit if:** you are an expert, want to build an audience and trust, and are ready to publish regularly.\
**Not a good fit if:** you need customers right now — content takes time.

## Service website with client portal: for working with customers

Booking, order tracking, documents, payments — everything in one place. This is not just a website, it is a tool for the business.

**Good fit if:** you have recurring customer processes that are currently handled manually or through chat apps.\
**Not a good fit if:** you are just starting out — this is a next step, not the first one.

## How do you choose the right type?

Not based on company size and not based on budget. Instead, based on one question:

**What should a person do when they come to my website?**

- Call → brochure website or company website.
- Buy right away → landing page or online store.
- Become convinced that you are an expert → blog or company website.
- Send an inquiry for a specific service → landing page.

## A common mistake I see

A company orders a beautiful website with many pages, invests time and money — and then it just sits there. Because nobody thought about how people would find it, or what they should do once they arrive.

A website is not a goal. It is a tool. And like any tool, it has to fit the job.

## Instead of a conclusion

Before you order a website, answer three questions:

- Who is my customer, and how do they find me? Maybe social media posts are enough?
- What should they do when they arrive on the website?
- How will I know that the website is working?
