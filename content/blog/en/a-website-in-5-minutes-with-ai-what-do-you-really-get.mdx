There are plenty of tools today that promise to build a website fast and without any hassle. Wix ADI, Squarespace AI, Framer AI, Durable.co – answer a few questions, and your site is ready in minutes. It sounds great, especially for a small business where every penny counts.

But what do you actually get? And when does it work – and when doesn’t it?

## What AI is genuinely good at

AI website builders can be genuinely useful in certain situations.

They put together a visual layout quickly, pick colours, suggest a page structure and fill it with placeholder text. If you just need to exist online, that’s often enough. A simple one-page site can be ready in minutes.

AI can also help you get started with writing. If you know what you want to say but don’t know how to begin – it can be a useful starting point.

## What AI does to your text – and why it becomes a problem

Here’s a paradox that doesn’t get talked about enough.

Ask AI to write SEO-optimised text, and it will. The right keywords in the right places, a clean structure, headings by the book.

But a different problem shows up: the text looks correct, yet by the second paragraph it’s hard to tell what the company actually does – or why anyone should choose them.

“We offer professional solutions for your business” – sound familiar? That sentence explains nothing. It doesn’t show what makes you different, doesn’t answer the customer’s question and doesn’t help anyone make a decision.

Visitors are starting to notice. Sites written entirely by AI have become recognisable: smooth text, all the right words – but a feeling that there’s no real person behind it.

And that affects what matters most: does the visitor stay on the page, do they understand the value of what you offer, and do they want to take the next step?

SEO and human writing aren’t opposites. But the gap between keywords and a real conversation with your customer still makes a big difference.

## What AI cannot do

AI doesn’t know your business. It knows templates and common patterns. But it doesn’t know why customers choose you – you have to explain that yourself. Without it, the texts come out too generic.

AI doesn’t connect everything automatically. Need a CRM, online booking, custom forms, a payment system or process automation? Then you’re limited to what the platform already has built in.

AI isn’t responsible for the result. If the site doesn’t bring in customers or is awkward to work with – that’s still your problem to solve.

## The hidden costs of a “quick” website

A website builder can seem cheaper at first. But look a little further, and the picture gets more complicated.

A paid plan with decent features costs roughly €20–60 a month. Over three years, that’s €700–2 000.

And the site stays locked inside the platform: moving it is limited, you usually have no access to the code, and some changes depend on what the service allows – not on what you want.

Template sites also start to look alike. That’s fine at the start. But over time it gets harder to stand out.

There’s also a factor that rarely gets mentioned at the beginning: the site might look good but not work as a tool. If visitors come but don’t become customers – the savings on launch stop looking so obvious.

## What no one warns you about – and what rarely gets thought about at the start

In recent years, a lot of tools have appeared that promise to build websites almost automatically. There’s also a new kind of developer that has emerged – so-called vibe coders – people who use AI tools to build sites for clients, fast and cheap. And it’s true – a lot has become quicker and easier.

But there are things that are hard to spot in a nice-looking mockup – and that cause problems later.

## GDPR is not just a page with some text on it

Does your site have a contact form, analytics, ad tracking or a chat widget? Then it’s collecting personal data.

Many people handle this formally: they add a standard privacy policy and consider the job done.

But what actually matters is: what data is being collected, where does it go, and who is responsible for it?

GDPR fines can be serious – up to 4% of annual turnover. But for a small business, the real problem is usually not the fine – it’s that the site owner simply doesn’t know how data is actually being handled on their site.

## Third-party services nobody thinks about

Even a simple website can load dozens of external services: analytics, fonts, widgets, tracking systems, CDN.

The visitor sees nothing. But every one of those connections affects speed, privacy and how much control you actually have over your site.

## Accessibility and technical requirements

Digital accessibility requirements in Europe are increasingly applying to private businesses too.

Enough contrast on text, keyboard navigation, alternative descriptions on images – things that rarely get thought about at launch, but that affect the quality of the site in the long run.

## Hosting and where your data lives

Where are your customers’ data physically stored? In the EU or outside it?

For many people this sounds like a technical detail. But in practice, that question can matter more than the design.

Professional work on a website isn’t just about how it looks. A large part of the decisions happen behind the scenes – and those are the ones that affect stability and security.

## When an AI website builder is a sensible choice

There are situations where a quick site really can be the right solution:

- you’re testing an idea and want to see if there’s demand before investing more
- you need a temporary “opening soon” page
- you run a micro-business and the goal is simply to show up on Google

In those cases, AI builders can work really well.

And it’s worth avoiding the opposite trap too: not every business needs an expensive custom project.

## When you need a professional

If your site needs to do more than just exist – if it needs to help the business grow, attract customers and automate processes – the work looks different.

An experienced consultant doesn’t just work on design. They help you understand your audience, ask the right questions, build the site structure around real business goals, help with the writing and think about how the site will work a year from now – not just how it looks on launch day.

The difference between a brochure site and a website that’s a real business tool is rarely about design.

It’s usually about understanding the task.

## To sum up

AI is a good tool – and I use it myself: to speed up processes, find ideas, write drafts and handle routine tasks.

It genuinely saves time.

But technology still doesn’t replace an understanding of a business, its audience and its goals.

If you need a website that actually does something – not just exists – it’s worth starting by figuring out what you really need. Sometimes one conversation is enough to avoid unnecessary costs and find the solution that actually fits your business.
