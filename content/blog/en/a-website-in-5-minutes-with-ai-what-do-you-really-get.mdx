There are many tools today that promise to build a website quickly and without hassle. Wix ADI, Squarespace AI, Framer AI, Durable.co — you answer a few questions, and within minutes the site is ready. It sounds appealing, especially for a small business where every krona counts.

But what do you actually get? And when does it work — and when does it not?

## What AI is actually good at

AI tools can genuinely be useful in some situations.

They can put together a visual layout quickly, choose colors, suggest a page structure, and fill in placeholder text. If your only goal is to have an online presence, that is often enough. A simple brochure site can be ready in just a few minutes.

AI can also help you get started with copy. If you know what you want to say but do not know how to begin, it can be a good starting point.

## What AI does to text — and why that becomes a problem

There is a paradox here that people rarely talk about.

If you ask AI to write SEO-optimized text, it will do exactly that. The right keywords in the right places, clean structure, headings that follow every rule.

But another problem often appears: the text looks correct, yet after the second paragraph it is still hard to understand what the company actually offers — and why someone should choose them.

"We offer professional solutions for your business" — does that sound familiar? That sentence explains nothing. It does not show what makes you different, it does not answer the customer’s questions, and it does not help anyone make a decision.

Visitors are starting to recognize this pattern. Pages written entirely by AI have become recognizable: smooth text, correct wording — but a sense that there is no real person behind it.

That affects what matters most: does the visitor stay on the page, understand the value of what you offer, and want to take the next step?

SEO and human, living text do not contradict each other. But the difference between keywords and a real conversation with the customer still matters a lot.

## What AI cannot do

AI does not know your business. It knows templates and common patterns. But it does not know why customers choose you — you have to explain that. Without it, the text becomes too generic.

AI does not connect everything automatically. Do you need a CRM system, online booking, custom forms, payment flows, or automation? Then you are limited by what the platform already has built in.

AI does not take responsibility for the result. If the site does not bring customers or is difficult to work with, the problem is still yours.

## The hidden costs of a “quick” website

A website builder can seem cheaper. But when you look further ahead, the picture becomes more complex.

A paid subscription with reasonable functionality usually costs around 200–600 SEK per month. Over three years, that becomes 7,000–20,000 SEK.

And the site is locked into the platform: moving it elsewhere is limited, you usually do not get access to the code, and some changes depend on what the service allows — not on what you want.

On top of that, template-based solutions start to look alike. That works at the beginning. But over time, it becomes harder to stand out.

And there is one factor that is rarely mentioned at the start: the site may look good but still fail as a business tool. If visitors come but do not become customers, the savings at launch no longer look so obvious.

## What nobody warns you about — and what is rarely considered early on

In recent years, many tools have appeared that promise to build websites almost automatically. A new type of web developer has also appeared — so-called vibe coders — people who use AI tools to build sites for clients quickly and cheaply. And that part is true: many things have become faster and easier.

But there are things that are hard to see in a nice mockup — and those are often what create problems later.

## GDPR is not just a page of text

Does the site have a contact form, analytics, ad tracking, or chat? Then it is collecting personal data.

Many people handle this formally: they add a standard privacy policy and consider the issue solved.

But what actually matters is this: what data is collected, where is it sent, and who is responsible for it?

GDPR penalties can be serious — up to 4% of annual turnover. But for a small business, the main issue is often not the fine. It is that the owner simply does not know how data handling on the site actually works.

## Third-party services nobody thinks about

Even a simple website can connect to dozens of external services: analytics, fonts, widgets, tracking systems, and CDNs.

Visitors do not see any of it. But every such connection affects speed, privacy, and how much control you actually have over your site.

## Accessibility and technical requirements

Digital accessibility requirements in Europe increasingly apply to private companies as well.

Sufficient text contrast, keyboard navigation, and alternative descriptions for images are things that are rarely considered at launch, but they affect the long-term quality of the site.

## Hosting and where your data is stored

Where is your customers’ data physically stored? In the EU or outside it?

To many people that sounds like a technical detail. But in practice, that question can be more important than the design.

Professional website work is not only about how the site looks. A large part of the important decisions are made behind the scenes — and those are exactly the ones that affect stability and security.

## When an AI website builder is a sensible choice

There are situations where a quick site really can be the right solution:

- you are testing an idea and want to see whether there is demand before investing more
- you need a temporary “coming soon” page
- you run a micro-business and the goal is simply to be visible on Google

In those cases, AI website builders can work very well.

And it is important not to fall into another trap: not every business needs an expensive custom project.

## When you need a professional

If the site is not just supposed to exist — but actually help the business grow, attract customers, and automate processes — then the work looks different.

An experienced consultant does not only work with design. They help you understand your audience, ask the right questions, build the structure around real business goals, help with the copy, and think about how the site should work a year from now — not just how it should look on launch day.

The difference between a brochure site and a website that is a real business tool is rarely decided by design alone.

More often, it comes down to understanding the task.

## Summary

AI is a good tool — and I use it myself in my work: to speed up processes, find ideas, draft text, and handle routine tasks.

It really does save time.

But technology still does not replace understanding a business, its audience, and its goals.

If you need a website that will be useful — not just exist — it is worth starting by clarifying what you actually need. Sometimes a single conversation is enough to avoid unnecessary costs and find the solution that truly fits your business.
