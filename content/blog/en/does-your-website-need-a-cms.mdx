When a small business owner orders a website, almost the same question comes up every time: "Should we use WordPress?" Or, slightly more modern: "Do we need a content management system?"

The honest answer is – not always. Here’s why.

---

## What Is a CMS and Why Does It Exist?

A CMS (Content Management System) is an interface that lets you edit your website yourself – change text, add articles, upload images. Without one, every change requires a developer.

The most common options are WordPress, Webflow, and Wix. They were created to give businesses independence from developers. And that’s genuinely valuable – but not in every situation.

---

## When a CMS Is the Right Choice

A CMS makes sense if you:

- publish new content regularly – articles, news, promotions
- run an online store with a frequently changing catalogue
- have multiple people managing content without technical skills
- want full independence from a developer for day-to-day updates

---

## When a CMS Just Gets in the Way

But there are websites where a CMS creates more problems than it solves.

**A landing page or business card site.** If you have 3–5 pages that change a couple of times a year – why add a system that needs to be updated, protected from attacks, and maintained?

**Custom or high-performance solutions.** WordPress under load requires extra configuration. Websites built with Next.js or static site generators are faster, cheaper to run, and virtually impossible to hack.

**When you rely on a developer for updates anyway.** If you always turn to a developer for changes – the CMS gives you nothing, but adds vulnerabilities and maintenance costs.

---

## What’s the Alternative?

The modern alternative is a **headless approach** or a fully static website. Content is stored in simple files or a lightweight headless CMS (such as Sanity or Contentful), while the website itself is fast, secure, and free from plugin dependencies.

That’s how **[interpol-check.me](https://interpol-check.me)** is built – a service with a database and complex logic – and **[itos.nu](https://itos.nu/en)**, the website you’re reading right now. Both run without WordPress, without unnecessary dependencies, and with no risk of being compromised through an outdated plugin.

---

## How Do You Choose?

Ask yourself three questions:

1. How often will you update the content yourself?
2. How important are speed and security to you?
3. Do you have the resources to maintain a CMS system?

If the answer to the first question is "rarely" or "never" – you probably don’t need a CMS.

---

## Conclusion

A CMS is a tool, not a standard. A good developer will suggest what fits your project – not what’s easiest to set up.
